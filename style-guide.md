# style guide, "the corporation."

a reference guide for keeping future pieces visually consistent.

---

## 1. core visual treatment

- **extreme contrast / threshold effect.** no image goes past two levels of lightness, everything's run through a hard threshold filter, or contrast and brightness pushed all the way.
- **photo-based, not drawn (but it can be).** the look comes from destroying a photo's contrast, using an editing technique called photobashing.
- **heavy edge noise.** silhouette edges are rough and break apart into smaller pieces, the usual smooth, bilinear edges get replaced by black and/or white speckles of varying size that read as hair, leaves, or rocks. a nice side effect of the threshold treatment.
- **grain/dither overlay.** characters typically get a gray noise or dither overlay so they don't read as totally flat, bayer (4x4) and stucki are the main dithering methods.

---

## 2. color (for characters)

- black, white, and one red, nothing else.
- red is used sparingly and always means something: blood, a wound, a hair bow, a pen. never used as an accent for large objects or surfaces.
- gray is allowed as a "behind" layer color, a flat gray silhouette of a person sitting behind the black elements on a white background, so the black shapes have something to pop against.
- no other colors, unless one is needed for a similar reason as gray, treat that as an exception, not a new rule.
- **outlines** are used to make a character pop from its environment. use the lasso tool for a more paper-like outline.

---

## 3. composition

- asymmetric layout with lots of negative space, most images have a subject on one side with a lot of solid black or white on the other.
- cropping is often tight to the subject's shoulders or chest, and/or background objects bleed out of the frame.

---

## 4. character design motifs

elements that can be reused or expanded on:

- **animal ears**, no buts.
- **uniforms**, collared shirts, ties, blazers, vests, belts, pants, **long** skirts, etc.
- **body horror / machinery hybrids**, a skeletal humanoid with an elongated torso, a starving humanoid on a screen, a security guard without a face. they should feel gross, but not overly bloody or detailed, more of a subtle sensation of revulsion.
- **express emotion with as few line changes as possible.**
- **blood** is used as a line detail, thin strokes of red suggesting a scratch, a torn piece of skin, a drip. *(only use blood to show a character's damage, don't put blood on creatures.)*

---

## 5. typography & branding

- lowercase sans/monospace titles, with little to no embellishment.
- title cards sit in black bars/boxes in a corner, kept separate from the artwork, and paired with the monogram logo.
- punctuation is used to build the title itself, the period after the title turns it into a full sentence, a statement on its own.

---

## 6. mood & tone

- **clinical, not grotesque.** the goal is unease and quiet horror, not outright gore or terror, dread comes from suggestion, not explicit depiction. **main goal!**
- **70s time setting**, 8mm film reel and video effects reinforce the era.

---

## 7. quick do's and don'ts

**do:**
- push contrast to the max before adding color.
- use red as an accent in one small detail only.
- keep edges rough and noisy.
- carry the same motifs across characters, animal ears, uniforms, confused/neutral resting faces, etc.

**don't:**
- use more than one additional color beyond black and white.
- center the subject.
- make red areas large or treat them as decoration, it should always read as a wound.
